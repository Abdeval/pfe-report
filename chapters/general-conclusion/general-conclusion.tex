\unnumberedchapter{General Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap-conclusion}

This thesis set out to personalize learning inside an existing LMS with
an LLM-based, context-aware multi-agent framework, while keeping the
decisions explainable and the teacher in control. The study of LMSs
showed that Moodle offers a reliable infrastructure and open integration
points, but models neither the learner nor the learner's context. The
state of the art showed that classical tutoring systems have
interpretable learner models but rigid content, that LLMs converse but
need grounding and pedagogical control, and that recent multi-agent
tutors rarely model the learner's situation, rarely run in a real LMS and
rarely measure learning.

The framework answers the question with one principle: \emph{code
decides, the language model writes, the teacher validates}. All agents
share one versioned model of the learner and of the learner's context,
fed by knowledge tracing and by the Context agent. Routing, grading,
mastery, recommendations, triggers and the risk of each student are
computed in code, and every decision carries its actual reasons. The
language model writes answers grounded in the course, quiz items and
drafts, all checked in code. The teacher reviews the questions, sets the
objectives, steers the recommendations, decides what to do with each
early warning and validates every change to the material. The platform
runs from Moodle through LTI~1.3, without any change to its core.

The evidence supports these choices within stated limits. On simulated
learners, the full framework gave a higher normalized learning gain than
the same agents without the context model and than a non-adaptive
baseline, and the evaluation itself corrected a ranking rule. The
Material Improvement detectors found the planted flaws of simulated
classes with high precision, and the early warning predicted, weeks
ahead, the learners who would end below the objectives far better than
the rule flags of the dashboard.

\section*{Limits}

The main limit is that these results come from simulation: the
simulator's assumptions decide them, and the value of the context model
depends on how much prerequisites matter in a course. The thresholds,
the style effect and the risk model were set or fitted on simulated
learners and one demo course. Moodle's logs are not read, because
Moodle~4.5 offers no web service for them and nothing is installed in
Moodle. Arabic is not yet supported, and BKT has not been validated
offline on a public dataset.

\section*{Future work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Run the pilot} with a real class, as the runbook describes,
        to measure the learning gain and the usability, and refit the
        risk model and the BKT parameters on real data.
  \item \emph{Arabic support} for the interface, the tutor and the
        quizzes, and an evaluation per language.
  \item \emph{Richer traces} through a Moodle log observer or the events
        of a newer version, and the device and affective dimensions of
        the context.
  \item \emph{A verifier agent} that checks every answer before delivery,
        and a measure of the hallucinations that cascade between agents.
  \item \emph{Institutional agents} for programme-level analytics and
        quality, built on the same shared state and the same early
        warning, and the use of the LTI roster and deep-linking services
        to reach other LMSs.
\end{itemize}
